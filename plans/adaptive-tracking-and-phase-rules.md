# Adaptive Tracking and Phase Rules

**Status:** Proposed rule set for the Personal Fitness OS user-zero prototype. These are conservative coaching defaults for validation, not medical diagnosis or a substitute for individualized professional judgment.

## The core loop

The product should operate as a repeating loop:

> **Plan → do → measure → compare with the expected range → hold or change one variable → explain why → set the next review date**

Most days should produce no major plan change. The system should respond to trends, meaningful symptoms, and repeated performance patterns rather than noise.

## What to track and how often

### Daily, in under one minute

- planned action completed, changed, or skipped;
- sleep duration and rested feeling;
- energy and unusual stress;
- muscle soreness and any pain;
- hunger or appetite outside the participant's ordinary range;
- alcohol or cannabis only when used;
- optional morning body weight.

Daily questions should be skippable. The app should collect the minimum needed to make the next decision.

### At every strength session

- exercise, resistance, repetitions, and sets;
- estimated repetitions remaining with sound form;
- one technique cue if needed;
- pain or unusual symptoms;
- whether performance was better, similar, or worse than the comparable prior session.

### Weekly

- average body weight from available measurements;
- planned versus completed activity outings;
- aerobic minutes and strength sessions;
- average sleep and rested feeling;
- adherence to the meal structure and approximate nutrition target;
- average hunger, energy, soreness, and enjoyment;
- schedule or logistics failures;
- menstrual-cycle observation when the participant chooses to track it.

### Every four weeks

- waist measurement under similar conditions;
- optional progress photos in the same location, lighting, clothing, distance, and pose;
- strength and cardiorespiratory progress;
- clothing fit, energy, confidence, and activity skills;
- phase decision: continue, maintain, reduce training, or change goal.

### Body-fat estimate

Measure no more often than every four to eight weeks for ordinary use, with the same device, time of day, hydration conditions, and protocol. Display the value as an estimate and emphasize the trend. Do not let a consumer body-fat reading automatically change calories or diagnose muscle loss.

### Clinical measurements

Blood pressure, laboratory testing, bone-density testing, deficiency assessment, pregnancy-related measures, and evaluation of symptoms belong with the appropriate professional. The app can store user-entered results and clinician instructions but should not invent interpretations.

## Weight trend rules

### Collection

When weighing is acceptable to the participant:

1. Weigh in the morning after using the bathroom and before eating or drinking, under similar conditions.
2. Collect three to seven measurements per week when practical.
3. Calculate the weekly average from the measurements that exist.
4. Compare weekly averages over at least two to four weeks.

Offer once-weekly or non-scale modes when frequent weighing creates distress or worsens eating behavior. Non-scale mode uses waist, clothing fit, photos, performance, energy, and adherence.

### Interpretation

- Treat the first one to two weeks of a nutrition change cautiously because water, glycogen, sodium, digestion, and menstrual-cycle timing can move scale weight quickly.
- Keep the plan when the multiweek trend is within the selected range and recovery is acceptable.
- Do not declare a plateau from one unchanged week.
- A practical plateau trigger is at least three weeks with no meaningful change in average weight or waist despite reasonably consistent execution.
- Before reducing food, check logging completeness, restaurant frequency, menstrual-cycle timing, constipation, sodium changes, activity, sleep, and whether the estimated target was actually followed.

## Choosing the current phase

The app should distinguish four phases: calibration, fat loss, maintenance, and muscle gain.

### 1. Calibration

Use calibration when information is incomplete, the participant is learning to track, recent intake is highly irregular, or the energy estimate has not been tested.

Default duration: **two weeks**.

During calibration:

- establish meal anchors and protein;
- learn actual portions and repeated meals;
- collect weight and schedule observations;
- begin manageable activity;
- avoid large calorie changes based on incomplete data.

### 2. Fat-loss phase

Choose fat loss when reducing fat is a current priority, the participant can follow a modest energy deficit, and health or recovery does not call for maintenance.

Default review rhythm: **weekly behavior review and four-week outcome review**.

A gradual population target is about one to two pounds per week, but a slower target is reasonable when preserving training performance, muscle, and adherence is important. The app should set an individual expected range rather than promise a date.

Adjustment rules after the initial water-change period:

- **Hold:** trend is within the planned range, strength is stable or improving, and recovery is acceptable.
- **Investigate execution:** trend is below the planned range but logging or plan completion is inconsistent.
- **Small deficit adjustment:** weight and waist are essentially unchanged for at least three weeks, execution is reasonably consistent, and fat loss remains the goal. Adjust approximately 100–150 calories per day or add a manageable amount of activity; do not change both automatically.
- **Increase food or reduce load:** loss repeatedly exceeds the selected range, or hunger, fatigue, sleep, performance, menstrual function, mood, or injury pattern suggests the plan is too aggressive.
- **Human review:** persistent symptoms, significant menstrual changes, suspected disordered eating, medication effects, or repeated inability to meet energy needs.

### 3. Maintenance phase

Maintenance is an active phase in which the goal is to keep average weight approximately stable while practicing the routine and recovering.

Use it:

- after a planned fat-loss block for reassessment;
- after roughly 5–10% body-weight reduction if a pause would help consolidate the routine;
- during illness, injury, major travel, exceptional stress, or disrupted sleep;
- when persistent hunger, fatigue, performance decline, or menstrual changes call for review;
- before deciding whether the next priority is further fat loss or muscle gain.

Default planned maintenance experiment: **two to four weeks**, adjusted to the reason for using it. Add calories gradually toward estimated maintenance and observe the weight trend for at least two weeks before fine-tuning.

Maintenance is not a failed cut. It tests whether the habits and current body weight can be supported without continuing the deficit.

### 4. Muscle-gain phase

Use a deliberate gaining phase when muscle growth and performance are the leading goals, training has been consistent, the participant is willing to accept slow scale gain, and further fat loss is not the current priority.

Beginners and people with substantial stored energy may gain strength and some muscle during maintenance or a modest deficit, so a surplus is not automatically necessary.

When a surplus is appropriate:

- begin around 100–200 calories above estimated maintenance;
- keep protein and progressive resistance training consistent;
- seek a very slow weight trend rather than rapid gain;
- review four weeks of average weight, waist, performance, and recovery;
- reduce the surplus when waist and weight rise without the intended performance trend.

## When to change calories

Recalculate after:

- roughly 10 pounds of sustained weight change;
- a formal phase change;
- a substantial change in weekly activity;
- three or more weeks outside the expected trend with reasonably consistent execution;
- pregnancy, breastfeeding, a medication change, a new diagnosis, or clinical instruction.

The app must show the old target, new target, reason, observations used, effective date, and next review date.

## Strength progression rules

### Progress

Increase the smallest practical amount of resistance or add planned repetitions when:

- all working sets reach the top of the repetition range;
- the participant reports the intended repetitions in reserve;
- technique remains stable;
- the same result occurs on two comparable sessions;
- no pain or recovery concern argues against progression.

### Hold

Repeat the same prescription when technique is still being learned, the repetition target was only just reached, sleep or stress temporarily affected the session, or the movement is improving without a load change.

### Regress or substitute

Reduce resistance, range, sets, or complexity when technique breaks down, the intended repetition range cannot be completed, or the exercise produces concerning pain. Substitutions outside the approved rule set should go to the trainer.

## Readiness and rest-day rules

The app should use a simple traffic-light check. It is a planning aid, not a medical screen.

### Green: follow the plan

- no new pain or illness;
- ordinary soreness that does not alter movement;
- energy, sleep, and motivation close to the participant's usual range;
- warm-up feels normal.

### Yellow: use the short or lighter version

Use a lower-load option when two or more are present:

- much less sleep than usual;
- soreness that noticeably restricts the intended movement;
- unusually high life stress or fatigue;
- warm-up resistance feels substantially harder than normal;
- performance has declined in a second comparable session;
- the full outing would compromise needed sleep or recovery.

The yellow option can reduce sets, resistance, intensity, or duration; change to technique practice; or use walking and gentle mobility. It should not become an improvised punishment workout.

### Red: stop and respond to the problem

Do not continue the planned workout with fever or acute illness, sharp or worsening pain, an injury that changes normal movement, chest pain, fainting, new severe dizziness, or unusual shortness of breath. Use urgent or routine clinical care according to the symptom and severity.

A rest day can still include ordinary movement when comfortable. Rest does not require remaining motionless.

## Deload rules

Do not force scheduled deloads on a low-volume beginner who is progressing and recovering well.

Trigger a trainer-reviewed lighter week when several signs persist across roughly a week or two:

- performance declines in two or more comparable sessions;
- ordinary loads feel unusually difficult;
- soreness or joint irritation does not resolve between sessions;
- sleep, motivation, or general fatigue has deteriorated;
- life stress has increased while training demand remains high.

A simple deload reduces working sets by roughly 30–50% and keeps resistance comfortably below failure for about one week. The trainer may instead reduce load, replace movements, or prescribe full rest depending on the cause. Persistent decline or symptoms need assessment rather than repeated deloads.

## Stretching and mobility rules

Stretching has different jobs at different times.

### Before activity

- begin with several minutes of easy activity;
- use controlled dynamic movements that resemble the coming activity;
- perform lighter practice sets before loaded strength movements;
- avoid using a long passive stretch as the entire warm-up.

### After activity or separately

- target the range of motion the participant actually needs;
- stretch a warm muscle to mild tension rather than pain;
- hold roughly 10–30 seconds and repeat as useful;
- schedule 5–10 minutes two or three days per week as a starting point.

### Adjust

Continue a mobility exercise when it improves the intended movement or feels useful. Change or remove it when there is no relevant limitation, it consumes time without benefit, or it causes pain. Persistent restriction or pain belongs with a qualified professional.

## Low-energy and recovery escalation

The app should notice clusters rather than diagnose them. Persistent fatigue, declining performance, repeated injury, disturbed sleep, significant menstrual change, severe food preoccupation, or escalating exercise used to compensate for eating can indicate inadequate recovery or energy availability.

When a cluster appears:

1. stop automatic calorie reductions and training increases;
2. recommend maintenance or reduced training pending review;
3. surface the relevant observations;
4. route the participant to an appropriate clinician and registered dietitian.

This matters especially when bone health is a concern because prolonged low energy availability can affect reproductive and musculoskeletal health.

## What the Progress screen should show

### Default summary

- current phase and days until review;
- weight trend range rather than today's weight alone;
- waist trend when available;
- completed versus planned movement;
- strength progression;
- sleep and recovery trend;
- the current system decision: **hold, progress, simplify, maintain, or request review**;
- one sentence explaining why.

### Expanded view

- raw measurements and conditions;
- trend method and time window;
- plan versions and target changes;
- measurement uncertainty;
- correlations presented as observations, not proof of cause;
- trainer or clinician instructions entered by the user;
- export and deletion controls.

## Example weekly decision

```text
DECISION: Hold the current plan for another week.

WHY:
- Your three-week average is moving within the intended range.
- You completed 3 of 4 planned sessions.
- Strength was stable and sleep was close to baseline.
- One higher weigh-in is within normal short-term variation.

NEXT REVIEW: Sunday after at least 3 morning weights.
```
