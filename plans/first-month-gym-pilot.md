# Validation plan: guided first month at the gym

Draft September 13, 2026. This is a proposed experiment, not an approved launch or work already performed. See the [research report](../research/2026-09-13-reddit-demand.md).

## Decision to resolve

Will U.S. women who feel lost starting strength training pay for a short guided experience that helps them use a plan in their gym, and can the trainer deliver it at a sustainable support cost?

Secondary question: does software or AI improve that experience beyond a good beginner program, short explanations, and access to the trainer?

## Milestone 1 — Understand the last real obstacle

Interview 12 potential customers: approximately four new beginners, four people who started but stalled, and four returning exercisers. Recruit beyond close friends; include people using free resources successfully. Confirm U.S. location, relevant experience, and access to a gym. Keep participation voluntary and obtain permission for any recording.

Use a recent concrete experience rather than asking whether an imagined AI coach sounds appealing:

1. Tell me about your last gym visit, or the last visit you intended to make.
2. What did you plan to do, and what actually happened?
3. Where did you become unsure, skip something, or stop? What did that cost you in time, money, or missed activity?
4. What did you try next: a trainer, friend, video, app, or AI? Show the resource if you are comfortable doing so.
5. What have you already paid for? What did you keep, cancel, or decide against, and why?
6. Which parts can you now handle independently? When would you still want another person present?
7. What would make a four-week introduction unnecessary for you?

Record the trigger, attempted solution, unresolved obstacle, spending history, and whether the participant fits the audience. Keep fitness/health details to what is needed for the research; do not collect diagnoses or medical records for market interviews.

Separately interview five trainers if the client-onboarding channel remains attractive. Ask for examples of repeated questions, current educational materials, preparation time, and existing software costs. Do not infer trainer buying intent from consumer interviews.

**Deliverable:** an anonymized interview matrix and a decision memo. A provisional go condition is at least six of the 12 customer interviews describing a recent, specific, unresolved obstacle that the proposed experience could reasonably address. This is a chosen operating threshold, not a statistically validated benchmark. Revise the audience or problem if the dominant obstacle is outside the product's control.

## Milestone 2 — Compare with a credible existing solution

Run six usability sessions. Choose an appropriate existing beginner program with the trainer; candidates for comparison include Before the Barbell or Caliber. Use public resources as intended and link to them rather than copying proprietary materials.

Have participants try to identify their next workout, understand the instructions, locate the relevant demonstration, and handle an unavailable machine or shortened visit. Observe what still confuses them. Any supervised physical exercise or form review should be handled by the qualified trainer.

Then present a small prototype: today's plan, short explanations, approved alternatives, a place to record questions, and a clearly described human-support route. Counterbalance presentation order where feasible. This small comparison is for identifying usability problems; practice effects, selection, and small sample size prevent strong efficacy claims.

Test three visible actions:

- **Explain this:** give a concise explanation and demonstration relevant to the current instruction.
- **This equipment is busy:** show a trainer-approved alternative for the plan.
- **My week changed:** help choose from approved schedule options without generating a conflicting new program.

Ask participants to explain the instruction in their own words and show the next action they would take. Record successful resolution, remaining uncertainty, time spent, and need for a human. Do not use time spent chatting as a success metric.

**Deliverable:** an obstacle log and revised prototype. Continue only if the prototype resolves recurring problems that the existing option left unresolved. If existing resources solve the problem, a curated orientation service may be enough.

## Milestone 3 — Run a paid, manually supported pilot

Offer 10–15 places in a four-week experience. **$49 is a starting price hypothesis for this experiment, not a market-derived recommendation or forecast.** It should be changed if actual delivery costs make it impractical. State the contents and start/end dates before accepting payment.

Proposed contents: short intake, trainer-approved starting plan, concise foundations materials, session reference cards, a weekly group question session, and a limited number of individual educational questions. Define those limits and response times precisely before launch. Individual form review is a separately scoped service; unlimited one-to-one coaching is not included by implication.

Use existing delivery and payment tools if and when the owner authorizes the launch. The research milestone does not set up payments, publish an offer, contact followers, or enroll anyone.

Compare two honest invitations to the same deliverable, keeping price and contents constant:

- “Feel lost at the gym? Get a guided first month.”
- “Want to start building stronger glutes? Learn the gym foundations with me.”

Both must accurately describe the delivered program. Randomize comparable exposure if feasible; otherwise call the result directional. Record qualified people exposed to each offer, clicks, purchases, and refunds. Do not divide sales by all 14,000 followers or interpret likes as demand. Separate new paying participants from existing clients receiving the program within coaching.

Measure:

| Question | Evidence to collect |
|---|---|
| Do people buy? | Actual paid enrollments, qualified offer exposure, acquisition channel, refund requests |
| Do they start? | Time to first planned session; reason for delay or dropout |
| Does the experience help? | Intended versus completed sessions; questions resolved; participant teach-back; self-rated uncertainty at start and end |
| What creates the value? | Use of static material, demonstrations, AI if tested, and human help; ask which component they would miss |
| Is delivery sustainable? | Total trainer and administrator minutes, including preparation, group time, follow-up, and escalations |
| What happens after graduation? | Choice to stop, continue programming, or buy human coaching; distinguish expressed interest from an actual purchase |

Suggested provisional decision thresholds: at least 10 paying participants, at least eight reaching their first planned session, and at least six able to describe a specific obstacle the service helped them resolve by week four. Report every dropout and the denominator. These are internal targets for learning, not industry benchmarks. Confidence ratings and attendance are self-reports unless independently observed; they do not establish clinical benefit or causation.

Check the economics explicitly. At $49, a participant who uses 30 minutes of trainer time at an assumed $60/hour leaves $19 before payment fees, software, acquisition, content production, administration, and taxes. That is not profit. Allocate group and preparation time across the actual paying cohort. If support costs exceed the price, change the delivery model or price before expanding.

**Deliverable:** a pilot report with purchases, completion, observed value, refunds, support cost, and a proceed/revise/stop decision. Do not build a subscription around first-month learning unless customers also buy a valuable continuing service.

## Milestone 4 — Automate only what earned its place

If the pilot supports the business, start with a small mobile-friendly web experience: intake, one current plan, short lesson cards, session notes, and help. Keep plans, approved substitutions, and progress facts structured and versioned. Avoid integrations until observed workflow needs justify them.

For an AI experiment, assemble a reviewed set of actual beginner questions. Compare short static answers with an assistant grounded in the trainer's approved material. Check factual consistency, arithmetic, conflicting instructions, clarity, and correct escalation to a person. Review unsupported answers before customers rely on the feature.

Proceed with AI only if it adds useful assistance at an acceptable cost and error rate. A successful course or guided service is a valid outcome even if custom AI software is unnecessary.
