# A simple proposal for helping her sell online

**Recommendation: she owns and runs her business; you build and support the agreed online sales setup and earn a sales-based fee.** No upfront development fee for the initial agreed scope. This replaces the earlier co-owner recommendation. These are proposed terms, not an agreement either person has accepted.

## The offer

| Item | Proposed starting term |
|---|---|
| Courses and agreed digital programs | **20% to you; 80% to her**, using the receipts definition below |
| New personal-training customers acquired through the site's booking funnel | **10% to you from the first package only**; no commission on later packages or renewals |
| Her existing coaching clients and unrelated/offline business | No share to you merely because she moves their payments onto the website |
| Duration | **12 months from launch**, with a check-in at 90 days; changes require both people's agreement |
| Initial scope | Up to **30 hours** to configure a simple site, checkout, course access, welcome emails, booking, and sales reporting using existing tools |
| Ongoing support | Up to **2 hours per month** of routine maintenance and small changes; additional work needs a separate agreement |
| Project costs | She pays approved domain, hosting, and project-specific tools directly where possible; personal subscriptions remain yours unless you agree a business-use allocation |
| Payment to you | Monthly, with a shared sales statement and payment within 15 days after month-end |

**The percentages and time caps are my suggested negotiation starting points, not researched industry standards or promises that the project will pay well.** She supplies and approves the fitness material and promotes the offer. You provide the specified technical setup and support. A large custom application, ongoing marketing management, course production, and unlimited customer support are separate work.

“Receipts” means money actually collected for an eligible offer after discounts, less taxes collected for remittance, refunds, chargebacks, and transaction-specific payment/platform fees. Do not subtract her personal pay, coaching time, general overhead, advertising, or your fee before calculating your percentage. This is a sales-based service fee, not a split of whatever profit is left.

List the eligible products and their fee category before launch. A labor-intensive coaching offer needs a price that still works after your fee. Each sale belongs to one category; do not charge both 20% and 10% on the same package. A course included in a coaching package has no invented standalone sale: use the agreed coaching-package rule. New products and mixed bundles need an agreed category before sale.

For coaching, “new” means not an existing client or already active prospect at launch. Record the booking inquiry and its connection to the site. Existing customers can still buy a newly launched standalone digital product; that product's 20% rule applies. For listed digital products, the fee applies to their agreed site checkout sales during the term, including renewals collected during that term. It does not establish a lifelong claim on those customers.

## What you would earn

These examples use eligible digital-product receipts **after** the deductions above. Her amount still has to cover her other business costs and taxes; your fee is before your own expenses and taxes.

| Eligible receipts in a month | Your 20% fee | Her 80% remainder |
|---|---:|---:|
| $500 | $100 | $400 |
| $2,000 | $400 | $1,600 |
| $5,000 | $1,000 | $4,000 |

An eligible first coaching package with $500 of receipts would instead produce a $50 fee to you. Low sales can mean very little pay for your setup work. Agree the scope before investing the time; choose this arrangement only if that contingent risk is acceptable.

## Keep the friendship and the handoff straightforward

Her business should control its domain, payments, customer accounts, and fitness content. The agreement should give her ownership of the commissioned site-specific work and continuing rights to any reusable components needed to operate it. You retain your pre-existing tools and unrelated code. Neither person should depend on using the other's personal login.

The proposed early-exit and buyout terms are in [BDR-0003](../decisions/0003-sales-based-setup-and-support.md). They give her a route to continue independently and protect you from an immediate replacement after an unpaid build. There is no automatic renewal or permanent revenue share after the first year.

Before starting, put the scope, fee calculation, eligible offers, ownership, reporting, and exit terms into a short services agreement. California worker classification depends on the actual working relationship; calling this revenue sharing or using a contractor agreement does not determine it. Have that point checked before using a commission-only arrangement. [California DIR guidance](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_independentcontractor.htm).

## A way to say it to her

> I'd love to help you get this selling online. I can set up the site and course delivery without a big upfront development bill. For the first year, I'd get 20% of the agreed digital-product sales and 10% of the first package for new training clients who book through the site. You keep your business and existing clients. We'd agree a small setup and support scope, you cover approved project costs, and we'd put an easy exit in writing. Does that feel like a fair starting point to discuss?

Helping for free can also be a deliberate gift. If you choose that, define the gift: for example, one afternoon configuring a booking page. Generosity does not require an indefinite support commitment.
