# U.S. Digital Fitness Market Gaps

**Strength training is the strongest first opportunity to test for this business. Public data establishes a large participation shortfall and substantial interest, but it does not establish the largest underserved market of paying digital customers.** Mobility and functional fitness deserve a direct comparison before committing to a full product.

The most comparable national measures put the 2024 strength-training shortfall at **67.1%**, versus **52.0%** for aerobic exercise. These are age-adjusted adult rates, calculated as the complements of published guideline-attainment rates.[^1][^2] A separate survey of people setting health and fitness resolutions for 2026 found closely clustered goals: strength **50%**, mobility/flexibility/posture **48%**, and mental-health benefits through activity **46%**. The published results do not establish a statistically clear winner between those goals.[^6]

**Weight management is a major competing opportunity:** Gallup found **52% of all U.S. adults wanted to lose weight**, while **26% were seriously trying**, in November 2025.[^34] Those all-adult results must not be confused with the fitness-resolution subgroup above. The case for strength is its specific opportunity and fit with this team, not proof that it has the broadest consumer interest.

For a small team with an existing trainer and an audience of women, my recommendation is to test **help adding strength training to an existing exercise routine**, alongside the previously proposed first-month gym program. Sell a concrete experience: a workable plan, instruction at the moment it is needed, help adapting to the person's circumstances, and bounded feedback from a trainer. Treat an AI assistant and a foundations course as delivery components whose value must be demonstrated.

This is a decision based on converging evidence and founder fit, not a measured national ranking of commercial gaps. The linked [primary research plan](primary-research-plan.md) specifies how to measure that missing commercial evidence. No survey, customer outreach, or paid experiment was conducted for this report.

## What was measured

The scope is U.S. adults and opportunities a small digital business could deliver in exercise, coaching, supporting nutrition, and recovery. Research was checked through September 13, 2026. Physical facilities, equipment manufacturing, prescribing, and clinical treatment are outside the proposed business model. Clinical and benefit-funded services remain relevant competitors.

Four different quantities are often incorrectly called a market gap:

| Quantity | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| A participation or health shortfall | People fall below a defined threshold or report a problem | They want a commercial solution |
| A desired outcome | People express a goal or intention | Existing solutions fail them |
| An unresolved service need | People want help and available alternatives do not meet their needs | They will buy this offer at this price |
| An underserved paid opportunity | An identifiable group buys a deliverable offer that existing alternatives inadequately serve | The business can acquire and retain them profitably at scale |

The national evidence is strongest in the first two rows. Some surveys identify barriers, and participation data establishes existing use of coaching. No common dataset located here jointly measures recent unresolved need, dissatisfaction with alternatives, digital suitability, and actual purchase across the candidate categories.

Consequently, there is no defensible dollar estimate for the largest commercial gap in this report. Multiplying an exercise shortfall by an assumed subscription price would manufacture a market size. Multiplying unrelated survey percentages would also assume relationships between behaviors that have not been measured.

## The comparable participation result

Healthy People 2030 provides the cleanest comparison: the same national survey, year, adult age range, and age adjustment. These measures concern self-reported leisure-time activity.[^1][^2][^3]

| 2024 U.S. adult measure | Meets guideline | Below guideline | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Muscle-strengthening activity | 32.9% | **67.1%** | Larger of the two core exercise shortfalls |
| Aerobic activity | 48.0% | **52.0%** | Substantial shortfall with a lower point estimate |
| Both components together | 26.4% | 73.6% | Missing at least one component; not a third independent audience |

The difference between the strength and aerobic shortfalls is **15.1 percentage points**. It is a descriptive difference; this report does not calculate a significance test without the relevant survey covariance. The combined measure does not mean 73.6% of adults never exercise. The groups overlap and cannot be added.

These age-adjusted percentages describe a standardized population, not the actual contemporary age distribution. They must not be multiplied by a census population to create counts. For comparison, NCHS's separate 2024 report gives an unadjusted aerobic attainment rate of **47.2%**, with a 95% confidence interval of **46.3–48.0%**. Its complement is **52.8%**, with an interval of **52.0–53.7%**. That is a different statistical basis, not a contradiction.[^32]

The business implication is narrower than the population result. Strength adoption merits investigation because many people are not doing it sufficiently. The survey does not tell us whether their obstacle is instruction, preference, time, equipment, disability, caregiving, money, or lack of interest. An educational product can address some of those obstacles, but cannot be credited with addressing all of them.

Nor does this establish that strength has the largest shortfall across every conceivable fitness outcome. There is no equivalent national guideline-attainment measure here for flexibility, sport-specific performance, body-image goals, or confidence using a gym. Their absence from this table is missing comparability, not evidence of a smaller market.

## Desired outcomes: strength and mobility belong in the same leading group

HFA's December 2025 Kantar survey covered 2,000 U.S. adults and estimated approximately 82 million adults would set health and fitness resolutions for 2026.[^6]

| Goal among those resolution-setters | Reported share | Approximate population equivalent* |
|---|---:|---:|
| Build muscle or strength | 50% | 41.0 million |
| Improve mobility, flexibility, or posture | 48% | 39.4 million |
| Improve mental health through physical activity | 46% | 37.7 million |

*Arithmetic using the publisher's rounded 82-million base. These overlapping goal populations are not unsatisfied customers, incremental app buyers, or additive market sizes. Exact subgroup bases and covariance are unavailable. Seasonal intentions may not persist. The same survey's roughly $61 monthly planned fitness budget covers the whole category, not a new app's price allowance.[^6]

This changes how the original idea should be tested. “Learn gym basics” is a possible route to an outcome. It is not the outcome most clearly measured in the national evidence. Recruitment should first establish what someone wants to achieve, then whether missing knowledge or practical support is obstructing that outcome.

Mobility deserves an equally credible offer in early testing. A strength-only survey would merely confirm that strength-interested respondents like strength products. Likewise, a question asking whether people would appreciate a personalized AI coach would reveal little about which unmet problem deserves investment.

## Comparison across the broader small-business opportunity set

The table below is a screening comparison, **not a numeric opportunity score**. Percentages have different denominators and cannot be sorted into a commercial league table. Rows also overlap: an older adult may want strength, mobility, and better sleep.

| Opportunity | Most useful measured signal | Existing supply or constraint | Decision for this business |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starting or restarting strength training | 67.1% standardized adult shortfall; strength is a leading stated goal | Ladder, Caliber, and free beginner programming already cover plans and instruction | **First test:** discover an unresolved execution problem worth paying to solve |
| Mobility and everyday physical function | Mobility/flexibility/posture is close to strength in the same goal survey | The goal combines several needs; pain treatment requires a different service | **Direct challenger:** test a specific functional outcome |
| Exercise consistency and mental well-being | 37% of U.S. respondents in McKinsey's survey reported difficulty motivating themselves to exercise | Sustained behavior change is difficult; reminders are not proof of durable benefit | Include in delivery; consider a standalone offer only if buyers select it |
| General weight management | 52% of adults wanted to lose weight; 26% were seriously trying | Desired weight change does not identify dissatisfaction; established nutrition, behavior, and clinical programs exist | Include in the national comparison; identify a specific underserved group |
| Fitness for older adults | AARP reports exercise barriers among 62% of adults 50+ | Accessibility needs vary; some have digital classes through Medicare benefits | Attractive segment if the team can reach and support it |
| Exercise and nutrition support during GLP-1 use | Gallup reports 11% of U.S. adults currently using GLP-1 drugs for weight loss | Noom now offers a free companion; WW also serves this audience | Conditional specialist test, not an empty software category |
| Practical nutrition and meal implementation | Historical survey barriers include food cost and preparation time; protein interest is widespread | A calculator cannot solve every affordability or household constraint | Test an implementation problem with the trainer's nutrition expertise |
| Sleep and recovery routines | 30.5% of adults reported short sleep in 2024 | Short sleep has varied causes; established digital sleep services exist | Meaningful need, weaker fit for this team's first independent product |
| Pain, rehabilitation, and adapted activity | 24.3% reported chronic pain in 2023 | Not all pain is exercise-addressable; clinician-led competitors exist | Requires a different delivery model and qualified partners |
| General fitness education or AI planning | Earlier Reddit work suggests confusion, but gives no national prevalence | Free instruction, workout apps, and trainer software already supply core features | Use within an outcome-led service; validate independent willingness to pay |
| Glute specialization, sport performance, or coach operations | No comparable national unmet-demand estimate established here | Narrow niches can work; generic coach software already exists | Keep as separate hypotheses, not measured losers |

Evidence for the rows is developed below. The initial priority reflects fit with the trainer and the available evidence. A different founder, referral channel, or professional background could rationally choose another category.

### Existing exercise customers are a useful starting point

HFA reports **81 million fitness-facility members in 2025**, using a population aged six and older. Only **4.6%** reportedly did not use their membership at all. In its separate 2024 study, **22.6%** of facility members used personal training and **32.3%** used small-group training.[^7][^8]

These findings establish a large existing service environment and undermine the premise that most gym members never attend. They do not establish satisfaction, prices paid, or demand for an additional digital service. The younger-inclusive membership population also differs from the adult NHIS denominator.

Commercially, people already taking action may be easier to serve than people whose entire exercise routine must first be established. That is an acquisition hypothesis, not a finding in the membership data. It is worth testing because it changes both the promise and the required support: “add strength to what you already do” is different from “change your whole lifestyle.”

### A measurable cohort aligned with the trainer

CDC's pooled 2022/2024 analysis estimates that **21.7% of women aged 18–44 met the aerobic guideline but not the strength guideline**, with a 95% confidence interval of **20.7–22.6%**. Applying the rounded proportion to the study's weighted population of 56,087,223 gives approximately **12.2 million women**.[^4][^5]

That is a population equivalent from the pooled study, not a 2026 census count or a count of prospective buyers. It does not establish gym membership, interest in strength training, or dissatisfaction with apps. Some people in the group may already strength-train below the measured threshold.

This is nevertheless a more specific research audience than “women who want to get fit.” Interviews can establish whether they actually want to add strength, what they have tried, and why existing help was insufficient. The friend's audience may contain this cohort; follower demographics and purchases have not been measured, so that overlap remains unknown.

The earlier [Reddit analysis](../2026-09-13-reddit-demand.md) supports investigating practical confusion and confidence. It does not estimate how common those barriers are. The national research broadens the hypothesis to include women who already exercise but have not established strength training; it does not invalidate beginner onboarding or establish which group will buy more.

### Older adults and functional fitness

AARP's April 2025 survey of 1,009 adults aged 50 and older found **62% reported barriers to exercise** and **65% barriers to sleep**. Its **75%** response about being likely to adopt brain-healthy behaviors with how-to instructions is broader than exercise and is not willingness to pay.[^9]

NCHS separately reports that **38.4% of adults aged 65 and older** met its aerobic measure in 2024. AARP's earlier work also suggests that awareness of exercise benefits alone does not remove practical barriers.[^32][^10] These findings support exploring accessible delivery, but do not quantify a market for one particular program.

The relevant offer might concern everyday tasks, confidence, or returning to activity after a long break. Those are different entry points from gym aesthetics. Delivery may need simpler interfaces, accessible demonstrations, and more human support. Those requirements should be observed in sessions with potential users rather than assumed from age.

SilverSneakers already offers online and on-demand activity through selected Medicare plans.[^22] A new business must determine whether its target customers are eligible, use those benefits, and still have unresolved needs. “Older adults are underserved” is too broad to serve as a competitive claim.

### Consistency and exercise for mental well-being

McKinsey's 2025 report includes a U.S. finding that **37%** find motivation to exercise challenging.[^11] It supports a recurring problem, but the answer could involve scheduling, social contact, enjoyable activities, affordable access, or human accountability rather than another app.

A randomized megastudy tested 54 digital interventions among 61,293 U.S. gym members. Forty-five percent of the interventions significantly increased weekly visits during the four-week program; only 8% produced significant effects after it ended.[^26] Those are shares of interventions, not shares of people helped. The study supports the possibility of digital assistance while warning against assuming durable effects.

For the proposed business, the implication is to measure what happens after initial enthusiasm. Course completion and app engagement are intermediate observations. The commercial and customer outcomes are continued participation, useful support, and a reason to stay or graduate successfully.

### Weight management and GLP-1 support

Gallup's November 2025 survey separates wanting to lose weight from seriously trying: **52% versus 26% of adults**.[^34] That is a 26-point difference between two reported measures, not proof that 26% are blocked by missing software. Reasons for not trying, current satisfaction, and purchase behavior require further measurement.

NCHS reports **40.3% obesity prevalence among adults aged 20 and older** in August 2021–August 2023, using measured height and weight.[^35] This is a health classification, not an instruction that every person in the group should buy a weight-loss service. It also has a different age base and observation period from the exercise estimates.

WeightWatchers already markets food, activity, behavioral support, and clinical options.[^36] General weight management therefore belongs in the comparison, but a broad audience is insufficient differentiation. A narrower routine, circumstance, or service failure would need to emerge from customer research. The recommendation for strength should change if a deliverable weight-management offer produces better evidence under comparable testing.

Gallup's revised September 2026 publication reports **11% current adult use for weight loss**, based on May–June 2026 fieldwork. KFF's late-2025 **12%** measure includes use for weight loss or chronic conditions. They have different questions and dates and should not be treated as a trend pair.[^15][^16]

Noom launched a free GLP-1 companion in June 2026, including nutrition tools, education, and strength workouts, available regardless of prescriber. WeightWatchers also supplies GLP-1-oriented support.[^17][^18] These are verified product-positioning observations, not endorsements of vendors' clinical claims.

This materially weakens the case for a generic GLP-1 companion as the clearest gap. A specialist service might still win through individualized exercise support or an established referral relationship. That would require evidence of a specific unserved need and suitable expertise. The trainer's credentials, clinical partnerships, and access to this audience have not been established here.

### Nutrition, sleep, and pain

IFIC reports **70%** trying to consume more protein in 2025. That measures interest and behavior, not deficiency or demand for a macro tool.[^30][^31] Cleveland Clinic's older survey identified perceived food cost (**46%**), preparation time (**23%**), and unfamiliarity with healthy cooking (**20%**) as barriers.[^25]

The potentially useful nutrition service therefore concerns applying advice to a real household: affordable meals, preferences, shopping, and routines. That remains a hypothesis. A broad educational course could help some people while leaving their binding time or cost constraint untouched.

Short sleep affected **30.5%** of adults in 2024, with a 95% confidence interval of **29.8–31.1%**.[^13] McKinsey's older wellness report described sleep as a particularly unmet area, which is relevant counterevidence to declaring strength the universal wellness winner.[^12] But neither establishes demand for this team's recovery product, and Sleepio already provides digital sleep improvement.[^24]

Chronic pain affected **24.3%** of adults in 2023; high-impact chronic pain affected **8.5%**, a subset of that total.[^14] Pain prevalence cannot be relabeled a mobility-app market. Hinge Health illustrates existing clinician-led digital care.[^27] A small general fitness business should distinguish ordinary functional goals from problems requiring a clinical service before deciding which users it can responsibly support.

## What the competitive review changes

Ladder already markets strength plans, exercise demonstrations, coaching, and tracking. Caliber markets a free workout tracker alongside planning and coaching options; Meg Squats advertises a free eight-week beginner program. Future advertises remote human coaching.[^19][^20][^21][^23] Feature availability was reviewed on vendor pages; complete subscriptions were not purchased or tested.

The proposed differentiation cannot rest on having a plan, video library, or a place to ask questions. It needs a documented failure of those alternatives for a specific customer. Examples worth investigating include translating instructions to unfamiliar gym equipment, recovering after missed sessions, or knowing when a trainer's judgment is needed. These are interview hypotheses, not claims that competitors never address them.

The same applies to software for trainers. Trainerize already offers programming, nutrition, habits, messaging, and AI-assisted features.[^28] Building an internal tool for the friend's workflow can be useful, but it does not establish a wider coach-software business. That would require separate research with professional buyers.

AI should therefore be evaluated on service quality and delivery cost. It might retrieve approved lessons, explain terminology, prepare check-in summaries, or route a question to the trainer. Each function should earn its place by reducing effort or improving the participant's experience. Generated medical conclusions are outside this product recommendation.

RevenueCat's 2026 report provides cross-category subscription benchmarks, but its selected global app population is not a national fitness-demand survey.[^29] Subscription retention must also be distinguished from exercise adherence: a person can keep paying without benefiting, or leave after successfully learning what they needed.

Trust belongs in the offer design. HFA's public survey reports widespread concern about membership changes and cancellation.[^33] State the service duration, trainer access, renewal terms, and exit route plainly. That is a product choice grounded in a reported industry concern, not evidence that easy cancellation alone creates demand.

## Recommended business experiment

**Test an assisted strength-start program before building a broad fitness AI product.** Recruit women who want to add or resume strength training, including people who already walk, run, or attend classes. Compare that offer with a credible mobility/everyday-function offer during broader research.

The first product can combine a short foundations sequence, one coherent training plan, practical adaptation to available equipment, and scheduled trainer feedback. Deliver foundational explanations when they support an immediate action. The course can still be sold separately or included with recurring coaching, but those are two different purchase decisions to validate.

The core promise should describe a participant outcome within the service's control: understanding and following an appropriate routine with support. Avoid promising a specific body transformation or assuming that consuming lessons produces behavior change. Let a consultation identify the person's goal, circumstances, previous attempts, and support needs before recommending the format.

Use existing tools for the first pilot. Record where participants get stuck, how long the trainer spends helping, which questions recur, and which materials resolve them. Those observations define useful software requirements. Automating an untested curriculum first would make it harder to tell whether a failure came from the offer, the teaching, the interface, or insufficient support.

The commercial measures should include paid conversion, refunds, attendance or completed sessions, trainer time, and contribution after variable delivery costs. Collect reasons for declining as carefully as testimonials. Include follow-up after the supported period; a successful short course may produce graduation rather than a justified indefinite subscription.

The 14,000-follower audience is a possible distribution advantage reported by the user. It is not 14,000 qualified leads. Measure actual reach, audience location, relevant goals, offer exposure, and purchases before estimating revenue. A small profitable audience can be enough; proving the largest national market is not a prerequisite to serving it.

The recommendation would change if mobility attracts more paid interest under comparable exposure, if strength prospects are satisfied with existing options, or if required trainer time makes the offer uneconomic. A referral-led specialist opportunity could also outrank the current recommendation if access and professional capability are demonstrated.

## Evidence limits and reproducibility

This is structured secondary research, not an exhaustive census of all products or a newly fielded representative survey. National survey estimates received more weight for prevalence than Reddit posts or vendor claims. Vendor pages establish what is advertised; they do not establish satisfaction or efficacy. The earlier Reddit sample remains qualitative and search-selected.

The [source ledger](sources.json) records 36 sources, observation periods, methods, and limitations. The [measurement ledger](measurements.json) records 39 numeric observations with denominators and available confidence intervals. [calculate.py](calculate.py) reproduces the descriptive arithmetic in [calculated-results.json](calculated-results.json). Unknown commercial demand is deliberately represented as `null`, not zero.

The CDC women's article contains a sample-size discrepancy: the abstract states 10,981, while the methods and tables identify 10,891 after exclusions. This report follows the methods and table base. It also follows the corrected activity-category labels and uses the direct published strength-attainment estimate when needed rather than adding rounded categories.[^4][^5] The approximate 12.2-million population equivalent is not presented with an invented count confidence interval.

No inference combines age-adjusted and crude rates as though they share a denominator. Overlapping goals and populations are not summed. Historical nutrition and behavioral-study findings retain their dates. No competitor-count ratio or analyst-assigned score is presented as a measured supply gap.

**What is established:** strength is the larger measured shortfall between the two comparable core exercise components, and strength and mobility are prominent desired outcomes. **What remains unestablished:** which specific digital offer serves the most dissatisfied, reachable people who will pay enough to support a sustainable business. The next evidence required is joint customer measurement and an actual purchase test, specified in the companion plan.
