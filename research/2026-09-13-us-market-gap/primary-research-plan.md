# Measuring Unmet Demand for a Small Digital Fitness Business

This is a prepared research instrument and analysis plan. **It has not been fielded.** It follows the [secondary market assessment](report.md) and is designed to distinguish a large health need from an underserved paying audience.

## Decision and scope

Determine which customer outcome should receive the first commercial test, then which tested offer produces the strongest customer results and contribution under realistic delivery constraints. The population is U.S. adults; findings from the trainer's followers must be reported separately from national research.

Do not ask respondents to endorse a product before establishing their actual problem. Do not compare “a personalized AI transformation coach” with “a stretching course”: unequal promises make the result uninterpretable.

Use these six outcome categories in randomized order:

1. Start or restart strength training.
2. Improve mobility and ability to perform everyday physical activities.
3. Establish a consistent exercise routine for energy or well-being.
4. Put practical nutrition and meal routines into action.
5. Improve everyday sleep and recovery routines.
6. Manage body weight through sustainable eating and activity routines.

Also include “another fitness-related outcome” and “none.” Record the open response before offering examples. Glute-specific or sport-specific goals can appear in the open responses and be coded separately. Ask weight-management respondents whether prescribed medication is relevant to their support needs, with a prefer-not-to-answer option; analyze that group separately. This six-category test cannot establish a universal ranking of every fitness niche.

Age, gender, current activity, and medication relevance are segment variables, not mutually exclusive product categories. Clinical treatment needs require separate assessment and should not be counted automatically as suitable for a general fitness service.

## Sampling

For a population estimate, commission a U.S. adult probability panel with a documented sampling frame, nonresponse treatment, weighting, and survey-design information. Begin planning around **1,200 completed responses**, then adjust based on the incidence of the candidate audiences and the desired precision. No panel has been commissioned and no cost estimate is assumed.

Under simple random sampling, a 50% estimate at n=1,200 has an approximate 95% margin of error of **2.8 percentage points**, calculated as `1.96 × sqrt(0.5 × 0.5 / 1200)`. This is only a planning illustration. Weighting, clustering, nonresponse, and smaller subgroup bases can materially reduce precision. It is not a promised margin for an opt-in survey.

A weighted opt-in panel can provide a cheaper directional comparison, but its results must be labeled accordingly. Demographic quotas do not turn convenience recruitment into probability sampling. Followers, Reddit volunteers, and current clients are separate convenience samples and must not be used to estimate national incidence.

Include adults across income, age, gender, geography, activity levels, and disability/access needs. Measure internet and device access rather than silently treating everyone as digitally eligible. A digital-only sample supports conclusions about its covered population, not all U.S. adults.

If the medication-related or another subgroup is too small, recruit a separately labeled supplemental sample. Do not pool an oversample into national percentages without appropriate design weights. Obtain a minimum useful subgroup size and detectable-effect calculation before commissioning it.

## Neutral questionnaire

Use plain language and a mobile-accessible survey. Allow “prefer not to answer” on sensitive questions. Avoid collecting diagnoses, drug names, or other health detail that is unnecessary for the business question. Pilot the wording with a small diverse group for comprehension; those pilot responses are not part of the main estimates.

### A. Current situation

1. Are you 18 or older and currently living in the United States? Terminate ineligible respondents.
2. In the last 30 days, what forms of exercise or physical activity, if any, have you done? Allow multiple selections, a free response, and none. Ask frequency separately for strength activity and other activity. These brief questions are for segmentation, not a replacement for the validated NHIS classification.
3. Do you currently use a gym, classes, an app, online videos, a personal trainer, a group program, or another source of fitness help? Include no help and self-directed activity.
4. What, if anything, would you most like to change about your fitness or related routines over the next three months? Open response first.
5. Which of the six outcome categories are relevant to you? Include another outcome and none. For each relevant category, ask importance from 1, “not important,” to 5, “extremely important.”

### B. Recent unresolved problems

Ask the following for each relevant outcome; keep category order randomized. If burden requires sampling outcomes within respondent, predefine the selection probabilities and analysis weights rather than selecting only the respondent's favorite.

6. During the last 90 days, how often did a difficulty prevent you from doing what you wanted in this area? Options: never; on one occasion; during 2–3 separate weeks; during 4–7 weeks; during 8 or more weeks; unsure.
7. Describe the most recent occasion. What were you trying to do, what got in the way, and what happened next? Open response.
8. What contributed to that difficulty? Randomize choices: uncertainty about what to do; uncertainty about technique; lack of time; cost; access or equipment; energy; enjoyment; confidence around others; conflicting advice; scheduling or caregiving; physical limitations or symptoms; difficulty continuing after a break; something else; unsure. Do not assume the listed obstacle is solvable by an app.
9. What have you tried for this problem during the last 90 days? Record named products or services, free resources, help from people, self-directed attempts, and nothing tried.
10. Overall, how satisfied are you with the help you currently have for this outcome? Very dissatisfied; somewhat dissatisfied; neither; somewhat satisfied; very satisfied; no current help; unsure.
11. How much of the original problem remains? None; a little; about half; most; all; unsure.
12. If you have not sought help, what is the main reason? No need for help; did not know where to look; no suitable option found; cost; prefer to handle it myself; not ready to act; another reason; unsure.

### C. Spending, access, and readiness

13. In the last 90 days, have you personally paid for help with this particular outcome? Record yes/no, category, actual amount or spend band, and whether it was a one-time or recurring payment. Distinguish employer/insurer-funded services and shared household purchases.
14. Have you stopped paying for any help in this area during the last 12 months? What was it, and what was the main reason? Include successful completion, cost, low use, poor results, poor experience, and another reason.
15. Which delivery formats would you realistically use during the next month? Self-paced lessons; guided app; live group sessions; individual remote support; in-person help; none. Follow with device access, reliable internet, and accessibility requirements.
16. What time could you realistically set aside each week for the activity itself, and separately for lessons or check-ins? Include no time available and unsure.
17. Do you intend to take any action toward this outcome in the next 30 days? Definitely; probably; unsure; probably not; definitely not. Ask what the next action would be before showing a product.
18. Collect demographic and channel information needed for weighting and segment analysis, plus whether the respondent already follows or buys from the trainer. Keep that relationship available as a separate analysis variable.

## Predeclared definitions

These thresholds are analytic choices, not established clinical or commercial standards. Register them before reviewing category results. Publish sensitivity analyses using adjacent thresholds so the conclusion cannot depend on one convenient cutoff.

| Metric | Respondent-level definition |
|---|---|
| Important recent unresolved need | Importance 4–5; difficulty in at least two separate weeks within 90 days; at least half the problem remains |
| Dissatisfied existing user | Meets the need definition; has current help; satisfaction is very or somewhat dissatisfied |
| Unserved help-seeker | Meets the need definition; no current help; reports searching/trying without finding suitable help or being unable to afford it |
| Other unresolved need | Meets the need definition but does not meet either service-gap definition; report separately |
| Digitally suitable stated audience | Meets one of the service-gap definitions and reports usable access plus willingness to use a proposed digital format; actual suitability still needs onboarding verification |
| Purchase intent | Response to a specific offer and price; never label it a purchase |
| Observed purchase | Completed real payment under stated delivery/refund terms; record refunds and net purchases separately |

Report dissatisfied users and unserved help-seekers separately before combining their nonoverlapping definitions. Someone who cannot afford current help is not automatically able to afford the proposed product. A respondent who does not want help must not be recoded as a dissatisfied buyer.

For each outcome, estimate the weighted share of all covered adults meeting each definition and the share within that outcome's interested population. Show both denominators, unweighted sample sizes, effective sample sizes where available, and uncertainty intervals.

Compute joint indicators from the same respondents. Do not multiply the overall national strength shortfall by a separate dissatisfaction rate and a separate intention-to-buy rate. Do not sum overlapping outcome audiences. If estimating people, apply suitable population weights or a compatible population base, not an age-adjusted activity percentage.

For comparisons, account for respondents answering multiple categories. Use survey-design variance estimation or a respondent-level bootstrap appropriate to the sample and weights. Predefine primary contrasts and a multiple-comparison method, such as Holm adjustment. Allow an inconclusive or tied result. A two-point lead is not automatically a winner.

## From survey interest to a real purchase comparison

Select the strongest few unresolved outcomes using the predeclared measures and inspect what current alternatives fail to do. Create offers that the team can actually deliver. Keep delivery length, amount of human support, presentation quality, and price comparable in the initial concept comparison; record any unavoidable differences.

A neutral common structure is: “A four-week program to help you [specific outcome], with an initial consultation, a plan matched to your circumstances, short instructional materials, and one scheduled weekly feedback opportunity.” This is a test specification, not a published offer. Set the real price after estimating delivery time and costs. Do not invent a market-clearing price from national fitness spending averages.

Randomly show each survey participant one appropriate concept and price, including a clear no-interest option. Report concept interest separately from the incidence of unresolved need. A restricted comparison among people eligible for all offers estimates relative appeal within that shared group; it does not estimate each offer's national audience. For population-wide results, account for both eligibility and response.

Run a separate purchase pilot with prospects who independently consent to be contacted or arrive through an authorized campaign. Make actual availability, dates, trainer support, payment, renewal, and refund terms explicit. Deliver what is sold. Do not use a fake checkout or suggest a program exists when it does not.

Start with a feasibility pilot to learn conversion and delivery variability. Then calculate sample size for a decision-relevant conversion or contribution difference. A handful of purchases is useful operational evidence, not enough to declare a statistically established winner. Randomize comparable eligible traffic where possible; do not compare a warm follower audience for one offer with cold advertising for another and attribute the difference to the product.

Track these fields by assigned offer and acquisition source:

| Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| Offer exposure | Unique eligible prospects assigned to and shown the offer |
| Paid conversion | Completed purchases divided by assigned eligible prospects shown the offer |
| Net paid conversion | Purchases remaining after the declared refund window divided by that same denominator |
| Delivery burden | Trainer minutes, support minutes, and other variable costs per participant |
| Customer behavior | Participation and the promised outcome assessed with the same procedure across suitable offers |
| Continued value | Outcomes after support ends; renewal or appropriate successful graduation |
| Contribution per exposed prospect | Collected revenue minus refunds, payment charges, variable delivery costs, and attributable acquisition spend, divided by exposure |

Report uncertainty for conversion and contribution, not just the point estimates. Track trainer capacity as a real constraint. Also report retention and outcomes for everyone who purchased, including missing follow-up; a completers-only result can be misleading.

If the goal is to estimate potential total opportunity, the eligible unresolved audience, attainable reach, price-specific purchase behavior, delivery capacity, and economics all matter. A product can have the biggest need population while another produces a better small business. Do not collapse those tradeoffs into an opaque weighted score.

## Decision outputs

Produce two conclusions: **which outcome has the highest measured service-gap incidence in the sampled population**, and **which tested offer has the strongest observed commercial results under its tested conditions**. They need not be the same.

State the population, field dates, sample design, exact offers, prices, channels, and uncertainty with every ranking. “Largest measured among these outcomes and tested conditions” is defensible when supported. “Largest gap in all fitness” requires a breadth and level of evidence this study does not promise.

If the leading alternatives remain tied, choose using delivery capability, audience access, and the smallest reversible experiment, while preserving the statistical uncertainty. If purchases are weak or support costs are too high, revise or stop before building a broad software platform.
