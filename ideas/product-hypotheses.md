# Product hypotheses

Updated September 13, 2026. Evidence IDs refer to the [source ledger](../research/2026-09-13-reddit-evidence.csv). These are proposed experiments, not confirmed requirements.

| Idea | Status | Evidence and uncertainty | Next test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guided first month in a real gym | Test first | R01–R09 show uncertainty choosing and executing a plan, including after months of attendance. Existing free programs are strong alternatives. | Observe beginners using a good existing program; identify what still prevents completion. |
| Short explanations attached to today's workout | Include in prototype | R02, R24, R26, R27: people want to understand the instruction and rationale. Incorrect explanations actively damage trust. | Ask users to explain the instruction back and then apply it. Compare a short card with chat. |
| Equipment-busy and short-on-time alternatives | Include in prototype | R06–R08, R18: practical constraints interrupt the plan. Other apps already offer substitutions. | Watch whether a small trainer-approved alternative list resolves the interruption. |
| Beginner foundations handbook/course | Supporting content | R01, R05: conflicting information creates overload. Caliber and Before the Barbell already cover much of the content. | Test a minimal orientation plus optional reference material; record what participants actually consult. |
| Glute-focused entry point for women | Test positioning | Fits the trainer's stated expertise and audience. S01 is only supplemental Reddit support; this scan cannot rank overall glute demand. | Compare messages with her actual U.S. followers, keeping offer, price, and delivery constant. |
| Nutrition without compulsory food logging | Small supporting module | R10–R14: logging can feel burdensome or intrusive. Some people value it, so preserve user choice. | Ask what people already do and which food decision they struggle with; avoid building another tracker prematurely. |
| Trainer onboarding and between-session education | Test alongside consumer pilot | R20: a paying client wanted teaching and a plan for independent sessions. R21: one trainer describes repeated basic questions. B2B willingness to pay is untested. | Interview five trainers and examine repeated questions and preparation time with permission. |
| AI answers grounded in the trainer's approved material | Conditional | R05 and R23 show existing DIY AI substitutes; R26–R27 show both interest and distrust. AI alone is not a differentiator. | Compare approved static answers with an assistant on real beginner questions; track unsupported answers and human escalations. |
| Autonomous form assessment, injury advice, deficiency detection | Outside proposed MVP | Users want reassurance, but this research does not validate automated assessment or clinical capabilities. | Keep exercise feedback with a qualified human and clinical questions with the appropriate clinician. |
| General all-in-one fitness subscription | Defer | Strong incumbents cover plans, tracking, videos, explanations, and coaching. Recurring value after graduation is unknown. | Revisit only if the pilot reveals a recurring unmet task and repeat purchases. |

## Working promise

“Walk into your gym knowing what to do, understand why you're doing it, and have help when you get stuck.”

This is draft positioning. Do not promise a particular physique, medical outcome, or guaranteed confidence change.

## Open decisions

- Is the best buyer a beginner, a returning exerciser, or a trainer onboarding clients?
- Does the friend’s audience contain enough beginners to support the offer?
- Is glute-focused messaging a better invitation than gym confidence or strength foundations?
- Which questions need a person, and how much support can the price sustain?
- Do customers want to graduate after four weeks or continue buying a distinct service?
