# BDR-0001: Validate a supported strength-start program

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Status | **Proposed**; recommendation prepared for both collaborators |
| Date | September 13, 2026 |
| Decision-makers | The trainer and the engineer, jointly; acceptance not recorded |
| Scope | First joint fitness product and validation sequence |
| Related decision | [BDR-0002: Founder compensation and ownership](0002-founder-compensation-and-ownership.md) |
| Review point | After the first paid cohort and at the end of an agreed 90-day pilot, whichever comes first |

## Context

The proposed team combines a personal trainer with nutrition expertise, a reported audience of approximately 14,000 followers, and experience training women, with a software engineer interested in building products and earning income online. Both are based in Anaheim, California. The trainer has particular expertise in glute development. Audience purchasing behavior, available weekly hours, business structure, and joint commitments have not yet been established.

The original concept is a foundations course explaining how to use a gym, training basics, and what to expect from personal training. It could be sold separately and included with ongoing coaching. Subsequent discussion introduced AI assistance and the question of which fitness need offers the strongest commercial opportunity.

The [national market assessment](../research/2026-09-13-us-market-gap/report.md) and [Reddit research](../research/2026-09-13-reddit-demand.md) identify promising problems but do not establish paid demand for this product. The objective of this decision is to select a practical first test, not to declare the largest fitness market definitively solved.

## Proposed decision

Start with a **four-week supported strength-start program for women who want to begin or resume strength training**. Include women who already do aerobic exercise but have not established strength training. Test the problem and existing alternatives before offering a paid cohort.

Combine a short intake, a trainer-approved routine, concise foundations lessons, practical help using the plan, and explicitly bounded trainer support. Use existing tools for initial delivery. Decide the actual price from the support scope and costs, then test purchases; the earlier pilot's $49 figure is a low-support price hypothesis, not the price for every version of this program.

Treat the course as a reusable part of the service and a potential later standalone offer. Treat AI as a conditional delivery feature. Add recurring programming or support only if participants want and buy a distinct continuing benefit.

Use Anaheim for convenient observation and collaboration, while testing a remote delivery experience that can work for customers elsewhere. Count extra in-person help so it does not disguise a weak online experience.

## Justification

### There is a substantial strength participation shortfall

Comparable 2024 national age-adjusted adult measures put strength guideline attainment at 32.9% and aerobic attainment at 48.0%, giving shortfalls of 67.1% and 52.0%. This supports investigating strength adoption. It does not measure willingness to buy help. [HHS strength measure](https://odphp.health.gov/healthypeople/objectives-and-data/browse-objectives/physical-activity/increase-proportion-adults-who-do-enough-muscle-strengthening-activity-pa-04/data); [HHS aerobic measure](https://odphp.health.gov/healthypeople/objectives-and-data/browse-objectives/physical-activity/increase-proportion-adults-who-do-enough-aerobic-physical-activity-substantial-health-benefits-pa-02).

Mobility and weight management remain credible alternatives. In the research, strength and mobility were close in stated fitness goals, while general weight-loss interest covered a broader adult population under a different question. Choosing strength is a judgment about this team's first experiment, not proof of a statistically largest paid market.

### The proposed team can directly test the service

The trainer can shape and deliver instruction, and can choose to introduce the offer to her audience. The engineer can observe users, build a small delivery experience, and measure purchases and support effort. Those are complementary contributions. Neither expertise nor software skill guarantees sales, so both should participate in customer discovery and offer design.

### The product tests execution as well as information

The earlier Reddit research found recurring uncertainty about choosing a routine, understanding instructions, and managing real gym constraints. Its search-selected sample cannot establish prevalence. It does supply concrete behaviors to observe and questions to test.

Existing alternatives already offer instruction and plans: [Before the Barbell](https://megsquats.com/train), [Caliber](https://caliberstrong.com/workout-app/), and [Ladder](https://www.joinladder.com/). The proposed service must resolve a problem that remains after a customer tries a credible option. More content or an AI label alone is insufficient differentiation.

### A small paid cohort makes business assumptions observable

The first cohort can reveal whether people buy, whether they use the program, what requires human judgment, and how much each collaborator works. These observations guide the curriculum, price, support limits, and software requirements. Building a broad platform first would commit more effort before those assumptions are tested.

The supported format may be less scalable initially, but its delivery work creates direct learning about what can be standardized. A profitable service or course is an acceptable outcome even if the evidence never supports a standalone software business.

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Advantage | Why it is not the first recommendation | Evidence that could change the decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Record a comprehensive standalone course first | Reusable, potentially low delivery cost | Purchase demand and the necessary curriculum are untested; information may not be the binding problem | Prospects buy a clearly scoped course and succeed with limited support |
| Build a broad AI fitness application | Potential recurring software revenue | Existing feature supply is extensive; useful differentiation and retention are unproven | A frequent unresolved task emerges, and users pay for a prototype that resolves it |
| Offer only individual personal training | Directly uses the trainer's skill and existing business | Limited leverage for a shared digital product; engineer's contribution needs a defined economic role | Customers primarily need individual feedback and the service economics work for both |
| Lead with mobility or weight management | Strong consumer-interest signals | The current collaboration has a concrete strength-training starting point; other offers need equally specific positioning | Comparable discovery or purchase tests favor a deliverable alternative |
| Build software for other trainers | Professional buyers may have recurring operational needs | One trainer's workflow does not establish a broader software market | Independent trainers describe the same unmet task and pay to solve it |

## Consequences and tradeoffs

Human support must be scoped and priced; otherwise apparent sales may hide unpaid work. A course designed to help users become independent may have successful graduation rather than high subscription retention. The trainer's personal brand can help recruitment but creates dependence that must be addressed through agreed content and brand-use rights.

The product needs to work under ordinary remote conditions. Local participants and existing clients should be reported separately from new remote purchasers. No revenue forecast should divide sales by all followers or assume that everyone in a population shortfall is a potential buyer.

The collaborators' earnings must be part of the model. Follow BDR-0002 to distinguish expenses, pay for approved work, residual profit, and ownership. Do not build a joint course that produces only an unpriced benefit to one collaborator's separate business.

## Validation and review triggers

Follow the [first-month pilot plan](../plans/first-month-gym-pilot.md) for interviews, observation, a small paid cohort, and selective automation. Approximately ten paying participants is a learning target, not a statistically established success threshold. The [primary research plan](../research/2026-09-13-us-market-gap/primary-research-plan.md) describes what a broader measured demand comparison would require; a national survey is not a prerequisite to the initial cohort.

At review, record purchases and refunds, acquisition source, participation, specific obstacles resolved, both collaborators' hours, actual expenses, and contribution after delivery costs. Explain declines and dropouts as well as successes. Track whether participants graduate, buy continued support, or leave without the intended value.

Revisit or reject the decision if existing resources solve the problem, buyers mainly want a different outcome, trainer support makes the price impractical, remote participants need exceptional intervention, or either collaborator cannot commit to an essential role. Expand software only when repeated observations justify it.

## Acceptance and implementation

This record preserves the recommendation and its rationale. Both collaborators' acceptance, the initial budget, exact offer, time commitments, and launch date remain to be recorded. No payment collection, business formation, or external outreach is authorized or performed by creating this document.
