# BDR-0003: Help her business through a sales-based services arrangement

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Status | **Proposed**; revised recommendation following the engineer's clarification |
| Date | September 13, 2026 |
| Supersedes | [BDR-0002](0002-founder-compensation-and-ownership.md), which assumed continuing co-ownership and was never recorded as accepted |
| Decision-makers | Trainer and engineer; commercial terms still require both people's agreement |
| Plain-language proposal | [Website revenue-share proposal](../plans/website-revenue-share-proposal.md) |

## Context and correction

The engineer clarified that the intended role is to help set up the trainer's online business and receive a percentage of sales, while helping her earn more and retain her business. The engineer has not expressed a wish to take half of her company or commit to a continuing co-founder role.

BDR-0002 assumed a more extensive shared business than this clarification supports. Its ownership, equity, separate venture, and residual-profit mechanisms are therefore superseded as the recommendation. No previously executed agreement is being changed; none has been recorded.

The supported strength program in [BDR-0001](0001-supported-strength-program.md) remains a product hypothesis. It can be sold by her existing business, with the engineer paid for the defined setup and support. The earlier market research does not establish a fair commission rate.

## Proposed decision

Use a **sales-based services arrangement for 12 months from launch**, with no upfront development fee for a capped setup using existing tools. Start negotiations at 20% of eligible course/digital-program receipts and 10% of an eligible new coaching customer's first package. Existing coaching relationships and later coaching renewals are excluded.

The linked proposal defines the receipt deductions, eligible offers, monthly reporting, a 30-hour setup cap, and two hours of monthly routine support. These are suggested terms, not industry benchmarks or accepted commitments. The fee is contingent on sales; the engineer does not receive an automatic equity interest or share of all business profit under this proposal.

## Justification

The trainer supplies the audience relationship, fitness content, promotion, and service delivery. The engineer supplies a functioning sales and delivery setup and limited continuing support. Giving her most of the eligible receipts reflects her continuing business responsibilities, while a defined percentage gives the engineer a route to earn back unpaid setup effort and benefit if the launch works.

The proposed 20% is a negotiation anchor for this specific scope. It is not derived from follower count, subscription cost, or a representative survey of creator–developer contracts. A simpler setup might warrant a smaller fee; a more demanding operation needs a different scope and compensation model.

Personal training is priced differently because much of its value requires the trainer's repeated labor. A first-package fee can compensate the online acquisition setup while allowing her to retain the continuing client relationship. A checkout alone does not make an existing client newly acquired, so existing relationships must be identified at launch.

A receipt-based formula is easier to inspect than residual profit and does not depend on how she chooses her salary or overhead. It still requires accurate records of refunds, fees, and eligible products. The product price must leave enough for her actual delivery costs after the engineer's fee.

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Fit and tradeoff |
|---|---|
| Fixed setup fee plus maintenance fee | More predictable pay; preferable if she has the budget and the engineer does not want sales risk |
| A small paid setup fee plus a lower percentage | Shares initial risk; reasonable if a fully contingent fee feels too uncertain |
| Percentage of every transaction through the site | Easy to state but can charge for unrelated or existing coaching revenue merely moved online |
| Half of business profit and permanent equity | Appropriate only for a substantially different, ongoing co-owner relationship |
| A clearly bounded free favor | Valid if deliberately chosen; does not imply future maintenance, new builds, or support |

## Timing, exit, and protection for both people

Before the build, agree the deliverables, content handoff dates, and launch conditions. If the project does not launch, decide whether to stop or extend it before doing more work. Under this contingent proposal, no sales means no sales fee; there is no surprise retroactive invoice for the initial capped effort.

Review at 90 days for actual workload, sales, and her delivery margin. The review does not give either person a unilateral right to rewrite the first-year terms. At 12 months, the fee ends unless both agree to renew or replace it with a different support arrangement. No automatic customer-lifetime commission is proposed. Only receipts collected during the agreed term qualify, subject to later refund corrections.

**Suggested early-exit term:** allow either person to end services on 30 days' notice, with an orderly handoff. If she chooses to replace the engineer early and keep using the completed setup, offer a buyout of **$1,500 minus sales fees earned to date after refund corrections, with a floor of zero**. Settle any unpaid earned fees separately and count them in that offset so they are not charged twice. This is an illustrative starting buyout value for the proposed small setup, to be agreed before work starts; it is not an invoice or a researched fair-market valuation. Paying it ends future sales fees and future support obligations.

For example, after $600 of fees, that agreed buyout would be $900. After $1,800 of fees, it would be zero. This means the 20% arrangement can end early when she exercises the buyout; it is not a guaranteed year of commissions. The engineer should accept the proposed setup value deliberately or negotiate it before beginning.

If the engineer ends services early, or fails the agreed obligations after the agreement's reasonable notice/remedy process, no early-exit buyout is proposed; reconcile earned fees and hand off the delivered setup. If she genuinely shuts down the project, no future share or buyout is proposed while it remains closed. Define treatment of a subsequent restart during the original term so neither a renamed offer nor a short shutdown becomes a way to evade the agreed arrangement.

Do not disable her site or retain control of her domain to enforce payment. Record permitted access, backups, site-specific ownership, and continuing licences for necessary reusable components in the actual agreement. Moving the same agreed offer to a replacement checkout during an active term should be handled under the exit or amendment terms, not silently remove it from reporting.

## Accounting details and risk

Pay monthly from transaction records with product/category, receipt date, collected amount, tax, refund/chargeback, transaction fees, eligible base, and applicable rate. Minimize customer personal information in shared reports. Discounts reduce actual receipts; free course access is not a sale. One package cannot incur both commission categories.

Later refunds should reduce the fee previously earned on that transaction at the original rate. Do not deduct a reversal twice. Carry an overpayment forward or settle it at closure; do not make negative current-period eligible receipts disappear by resetting them to zero. Exclude general advertising and overhead from the receipt formula; review their effect on her margin separately.

Keep project-specific software costs approved and visible. The engineer's existing personal subscriptions are not automatically reimbursable in full. Additional work needs a new scope and compensation agreement. Revisit pricing or scope before a two-hour support commitment becomes a much larger unpaid role.

This arrangement can pay poorly if sales are low. As an illustration, $6,000 of eligible annual digital receipts produces $1,200 in fees. If the engineer uses all 30 setup hours and 24 support hours, that is about $22.22 per hour before the engineer's costs and taxes. The figure is not a forecast. The reward is contingent, and an agreed low-revenue support stop or early exit may be preferable to continuing indefinitely.

## Legal implementation

The intended arrangement is services paid by reference to sales; that label does not by itself determine employment, partnership, tax treatment, or ownership. California Corporations Code §16202 distinguishes gross-return sharing from co-ownership and contains exceptions for certain service compensation, while the actual conduct still matters. [California Corporations Code §16202](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CORP&sectionNum=16202.).

California's worker-classification rules likewise depend on the real relationship and any applicable exceptions. Have a California professional check the services agreement and classification before using contingent compensation; this proposal does not establish that commission-only pay would be lawful if the engineer were an employee. [California DIR guidance](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_independentcontractor.htm).

Keep the actual agreement short and suited to this scope. It should settle the fee base, eligible offers, work limits, launch dates, reporting, buyout, ownership, and handoff. This record recommends no new jointly owned entity, makes no business filing, and creates no accepted payment obligation. Sources checked September 13, 2026.
