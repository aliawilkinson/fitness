# BDR-0002: Separate expenses, work compensation, profit, and ownership

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Status | **Proposed commercial terms for discussion**; not an executed agreement |
| Date | September 13, 2026 |
| Decision-makers | The trainer and the engineer, jointly; acceptance not recorded |
| Applies to | Explicitly listed joint digital products and supported programs |
| Related decision | [BDR-0001: Supported strength-start program](0001-supported-strength-program.md) |
| Review point | After each cohort; formal continuation decision after a proposed 90-day pilot |

## Recommendation

**For a genuine collaboration in which both people remain active, start with a 50/50 split of the joint product's residual distributable profit, after approved costs, budgeted compensation for both people's work, and an agreed cash reserve.** Keep the trainer's existing business and both people's pre-existing assets outside the arrangement unless specifically licensed or contributed.

Use an agreed, limited pilot to test how you work together. If both want to continue as long-term co-owners, target 50/50 ownership of the new venture, with documented vesting or departure/buyback provisions. A pilot profit split does not itself specify all equity, voting, tax, intellectual-property, or exit rights. Those require the actual agreement and chosen structure.

This is a proposed starting point, not a market-derived valuation of either person and not a claim that every creator–engineer collaboration should be equal. It assumes both contribute ongoing work, take responsibility for the business, and share the risk within an agreed budget. Exact availability, cash contributions, and obligations must be discussed before adoption.

## Why this allocation is reasonable

The trainer contributes expertise, teaching, customer judgment, reputation, and possible access to an audience. The engineer contributes product development, technical delivery, automation, operations, and the ability to turn repeated service needs into reusable tools. Both should help understand customers and sell the offer.

The audience and technical skill are complementary inputs to a new business. There is no defensible formula here that converts 14,000 followers and a list of software subscriptions into an exact ownership ratio. An expensive technical build also does not establish customer value merely because it took time.

Equal residual profit is a reasonable default when both accept meaningful continuing responsibilities. Unequal recurring workload can be reflected in agreed work compensation; actual business expenses can be reimbursed. Those mechanisms reduce the need to renegotiate permanent ownership whenever one person's current task load changes.

Equal or near-equal equity is one established recommendation for committed co-founders; Y Combinator also recommends vesting. That advice concerns long-term startups and is a reference point, not proof that this particular small business needs venture-style terms. [YC: How to Split Equity Among Co-Founders](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/splitting-equity-among-founders).

## Four separate economic questions

| Layer | Proposed treatment | What it compensates |
|---|---|---|
| Reimbursement | Repay documented, mutually approved business costs once | Money advanced for the venture |
| Work compensation | Agree fixed fees or capped budgets for specified work before it starts, when the business can fund them | Delivery, content, engineering, sales, and operations actually commissioned |
| Residual profit distribution | Divide available residual cash 50/50 under the agreed rules | Continuing ownership/risk and the combined business result |
| Equity and voting | If continuing as co-owners, agree target ownership and governance in the governing documents | Long-term rights, including what happens on sale, departure, or closure |

These are business descriptions. The accountant must map payments and allocations to the actual entity and tax treatment. Do not assume that calling something a fee makes either owner an independent contractor or employee.

## Expenses and existing subscriptions

Reimburse incremental project costs, such as a dedicated domain, hosting, approved API usage, payment tools, or additional business seats. For a subscription already used personally or across several projects, agree a reasonable business-use allocation or reimburse only the additional cost caused by this project. The entire historical subscription bill is not automatically a joint-business expense.

Record provider, purpose, amount, billing period, payer, and approval. Count each cost once: paying a provider directly and reimbursing the person for that same invoice are not two expenses. Use business billing and permitted user access when needed; personal accounts and credentials are not assets transferred by this proposal.

Subscription spending is normally addressed as an expense or documented cash contribution, rather than an automatic entitlement to additional permanent equity. A material cash investment deserves a separate agreement stating whether it is capital or a loan. Do not quietly convert reimbursable expenses into an open-ended founder loan.

## Pay for work without an unlimited time bill

Before each cohort or work period, approve each person's deliverables, availability, support boundaries, fee or hourly cap, and payment timing. The trainer's work may include programming, lessons, promotion, intake, and feedback. The engineer's work may include the delivery flow, customer research, analytics, support, and maintenance.

Use a budget that the offer can support. Equal ownership does not require equal hourly rates or equal monthly pay. Equally, the engineer should not create unrequested features and invoice unlimited hours, and the trainer should not absorb unlimited customer support because it was never priced.

At the pre-revenue stage, explicitly distinguish unpaid, capped founder effort from paid work. Agree a pilot time and cash limit for each person. No unpaid work automatically becomes a debt, royalty, or additional equity unless separately agreed in writing. Do not commit to fees without a credible funding plan; if compensation is deferred, document the amount, priority, and conditions in advance.

Audience promotion is part of the trainer's agreed role under this model. Do not add a separate founder affiliate commission or royalty by default on top of the same promised contribution. If an additional campaign or asset genuinely requires separate compensation, both should approve it and avoid paying twice for the same work.

## What happens to customer revenue

Keep a written list of joint offers and their seller, price, delivery responsibilities, and payment destination. Use a dedicated business ledger and an appropriately established business bank/payment account. Give both collaborators access to the financial records.

| Customer transaction | Proposed treatment |
|---|---|
| Joint standalone course, supported cohort, or joint membership | All receipts enter the joint product ledger; deduct agreed costs and compensation, then apply the profit policy |
| Trainer's pre-existing independent coaching services | Remain her business, except for a separately agreed course licence or other service bought from the joint product |
| Joint course included with her independent coaching package | Her business pays the joint product a fixed onboarding licence fee per newly enrolled client; the customer may see it as included |
| Individual-coaching upsell sold through the joint product during the pilot | Default to a joint offer: venture receives payment, trainer receives the agreed delivery compensation, and residual profit follows the same policy |
| A genuinely separate referral arrangement | Use a distinct agreement approved before offering it; do not assume a share of every future coaching purchase |
| Engineer's unrelated consulting, tools, and other products | Remain outside the joint venture |

For the included-course model, a **$25 one-time licence per new coaching client** is a discussion starting point for self-paced course access only, not a researched market price. At ten new clients, her coaching business would pay the joint product $250. Choose the actual rate after reviewing course costs and value. Additional human support must be scoped and priced separately.

Specify access duration, support, refunds, eligible existing clients, and when a licence becomes payable. Both approve the related-business price. Count the licence payment as the joint venture's revenue, without also counting her whole coaching package as joint revenue. A small licence fee is not guaranteed to cover the cost of creating the course.

This directly supports the original concept: the course can be free to a coaching customer while still compensating the jointly built product. It also avoids giving either collaborator an undefined claim over the other's existing business.

## Cash allocation and a worked example

At each agreed financial close, start with collected receipts and reconcile refunds, chargebacks, taxes collected for remittance, actual expenses, reimbursements, and amounts owed. Provide for committed customer delivery, liabilities, and the agreed operating reserve before discretionary distributions. Pay approved work compensation according to its documented terms; neither person can unilaterally increase it.

The residual cash available after those requirements is the distribution pool. Split that pool 50/50. If there is no positive available pool, there is no residual distribution. Do not distribute customer prepayments that are still needed to deliver the program. This is a management cash policy, not a tax accounting formula or a rule overriding creditor obligations.

**Illustration only: the amounts below are assumptions, not a sales forecast, price recommendation, or estimate of either person's take-home income.** Assume the period's service has been delivered and there are no additional unpaid obligations.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Customer receipts, excluding any taxes collected for remittance | $5,000 |
| Refunds | −$250 |
| All approved third-party costs and reimbursements for this example | −$350 |
| Trainer's approved work compensation | −$1,000 |
| Engineer's approved work compensation | −$1,000 |
| Remaining operating cash | **$2,400** |
| Additional agreed business reserve | −$400 |
| Cash available for residual distribution | **$2,000** |
| Trainer's 50% distribution | **$1,000** |
| Engineer's 50% distribution | **$1,000** |

Each receives $2,000 in work compensation plus residual distributions combined, before personal taxes, and the business retains $400. Any reimbursement included in the cost line returns money previously spent; it is not additional earnings in this illustration. Actual entity, insurance, accounting, tax, acquisition, and software costs must be included when incurred or reserved for. The figures do not recover unspecified historical or unpaid startup work.

The same equal ownership could produce different personal receipts if, for example, the trainer delivers more paid sessions or the engineer performs a separately approved build. Keep the distinction visible: compensation pays for work; residual distributions follow the ownership/profit agreement.

## Ownership of material, software, brand, and customer relationships

Recommended boundaries to implement in the actual agreements:

- Each retains pre-existing materials, personal social accounts, general know-how, and unrelated work. List those assets explicitly. Followers are people and platform relationships, not a customer list automatically transferred to the venture.
- The venture receives the specific licences needed to use the trainer's existing lessons, name, likeness, and branding and the engineer's existing code or tools. Define scope, duration, updates, permitted reuse, and what survives departure.
- New jointly commissioned product materials and product-specific software should be owned by the venture through appropriate written assignments, subject to listed third-party licences and agreed reusable components.
- Joint product accounts and appropriately collected customer records should be administered for the venture, with suitable access for both. Using customer data in a separate business is a separate permission question, not an automatic founder right.
- Plan how to fulfill already sold access if either person leaves. Do not promise customers lifetime access or assume perpetual rights to someone's face, name, future teaching, or personal account.

These are proposed allocation terms, not a legal conclusion that current material is already jointly owned. Neither person should become dependent on the other's personal login to run an agreed joint product.

## Governance, departure, and review

Assign routine responsibilities within a jointly approved budget: trainer for training content and delivery standards; engineer for technical implementation and operations. Both approve the offer, spending outside the budget, compensation changes, profit distributions, debt, ownership changes, related-business licence prices, and sale or exclusive licensing of the product.

For a material disagreement, keep the existing agreed position while discussing it, then use a mutually selected mediator or advisor. If unresolved, follow an agreed buyout or orderly wind-down process. Do not use a token 51/49 split merely to avoid writing decision rules, or invent a forced-buyout price in this planning document.

For a 90-day pilot, define the start and end dates and do not imply automatic renewal. On ending it, first fulfill or refund customer commitments, reconcile outstanding expenses and compensation, and follow the agreed allocation of remaining assets and cash. Restrictions on future work and access rights need explicit terms; neither person automatically owns half of every future fitness or software project.

For a continuing company, my starting recommendation is 50/50 target ownership with mutual vesting or tailored buyback terms. Four-year vesting with a one-year cliff is a common venture-startup reference, described by YC; it should not be copied mechanically into a small LLC or a completed course project. Have counsel tailor the mechanism, any credit for pilot work, departure treatment, and associated tax steps. Earned work compensation and reimbursable expenses remain distinct from unvested ownership. [YC founder-equity guidance](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/splitting-equity-among-founders).

## When 50/50 is the wrong model

| Actual relationship | Better starting model |
|---|---|
| Both build, sell, operate, and remain responsible | The recommended co-owner model, with costs and work compensation separated |
| Engineer only builds a site and provides occasional maintenance | A defined project fee and maintenance arrangement; revenue-based compensation only if intentionally negotiated |
| Trainer provides a one-time content contribution or promotion and has little continuing role | A negotiated content licence and/or attributable sales commission, instead of assuming an equal operating partnership |
| One person commits materially more time, money, responsibility, or business assets for the long term | Revisit ownership and risk allocation explicitly; do not conceal the imbalance with arbitrary hours or undefined promises |

No percentage is proposed for those alternative relationships because their scope has not been established. Review actual contributions after each cohort, and renegotiate future work prospectively rather than rewriting past payments unilaterally.

## California implementation and tax treatment

For an ongoing business with two genuine owners, a jointly owned LLC is a reasonable structure to discuss with a California business attorney and CPA. The appropriate structure depends on the services, liability exposure, professional credentials, existing businesses, and tax circumstances. The SBA outlines how structure affects ownership, liability, and taxation; this record does not make that selection or file an entity. [SBA business structures](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/launch-your-business/).

Do not assume an informal pilot avoids partnership law. California Corporations Code section 16202 provides that carrying on a business as co-owners for profit can form a partnership regardless of intent, and sharing profits can create a presumption subject to specified exceptions. Set up the actual relationship before jointly operating and collecting payments. [California Corporations Code §16202](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CORP&sectionNum=16202.).

For an LLC taxed as a partnership, owners performing services are generally self-employed rather than W-2 employees of that partnership. “Work compensation” in this proposal must be classified correctly; it is not an instruction to put both owners on payroll or issue contractor forms. Taxable income allocations and cash distributions need separate accounting. [IRS: Paying yourself](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/paying-yourself); [IRS: Partnerships](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/partnerships); [IRS Publication 541](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p541).

California generally imposes an $800 annual tax on LLCs organized or doing business in the state, subject to stated exceptions and classification rules. The broad first-year exemption described by FTB covered tax years beginning in 2021–2023; do not assume it applies to a new 2026 LLC. Budget formation, annual obligations, and applicable fees using the actual structure. [California FTB: LLCs](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/business/types/limited-liability-company/index.html).

The attorney and CPA should convert the agreed commercial terms into the appropriate collaboration/operating documents, licences and assignments, and payment/tax procedures. This review is warranted because actual profit sharing, ownership, and California operations have legal and tax consequences; the BDR is the business brief for that work. Sources checked September 13, 2026.

## Terms still requiring both collaborators' agreement

The proposal is concrete enough to discuss but does not claim agreement on: the specific joint offers; time commitments; pilot cash and work limits; compensation budgets; reserve policy; course licence price and access scope; existing assets; entity and tax treatment; equity/vesting; or exit terms. Record those choices and acceptance separately. Saving or pushing this file does not execute them.
